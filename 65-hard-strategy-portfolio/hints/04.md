# Complexity

**Building the network is `O(n + m)`** — one arc per non-zero contribution, one per
prerequisite pair, each with its zero-capacity reverse. Nothing is sorted, nothing
is deduplicated, no closure is computed. The network has `n + 2` nodes and at most
`n + m` forward arcs.

**The flow is `O(V² E)`** in the textbook worst case, `V = n + 2` and `E = n + m`.
That bound is loose here and it is worth knowing why: Dinic's phase count is capped
by the length of the longest shortest route it ever uses, which is at most two more
than the longest prerequisite chain the flow actually threads. On the two seeded
recipes the whole run takes 22 and 41 phases, and 17150 and 14607 augmentations, for
networks of 30002 nodes / 97500 arcs and 20002 nodes / 46000 arcs. `golden/main.py`
finishes both in **Python** in 2.3 s and 1.3 s, so the intended solution is not
close to any budget in a compiled language.

**Space is `O(n + m)`**: two integers per arc plus a level and a cursor per node.
The route walk's own stack is bounded by the route length, which is `O(n)` on a
long prerequisite chain — the same reason `03.md` insists the walk be explicit
rather than recursive. Nothing here needs more than the input's own order of
magnitude.

## Why the obvious alternative loses, and by how much

**Enumerating the activation sets** is the baseline, and it is what
`rotten/main.py` does: walk every subset of the `n` strategies, discard the ones
that activate something without one of its prerequisites, keep the best total.
Correct for every input the README allows. Its cost is `2^n` candidates at
`O(n + m)` each, and `n` here is not 20 or 40.

| recipe | shape | n | m | candidates | answer | golden | naive, native |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `09_large_layered_feeds` | 4 layers of 7500, three prerequisites each, one shallow acyclic graph | 30000 | 67500 | `2^30000` ≈ 10^9031 | 2680154995065 | 2.3 s | ≈ 10^9023 s |
| `10_large_cyclic_clusters` | 4000 five-strategy prerequisite cycles in 4 ranks, two cross links each | 20000 | 26000 | `2^20000` ≈ 10^6021 | 1224186048043 | 1.3 s | ≈ 10^6012 s |

Measured with a temporary optimized native control on the two seeded inputs. The
control is a literal transcription of `rotten/main.py` — same candidate order, each
strategy's prerequisites precomputed into a 64-bit word, an early exit on the first
missing one, 64-bit totals — compiled `cc -O3 -march=native -funroll-loops`, and
checked against seven of the tracked fixtures to confirm it answers what
`golden/main.py` answers. Because a 30000-strategy candidate does not fit in any
machine word, the control was pointed at the first `2^30` candidates of the real
input — which are exactly the subsets of strategies `0 .. 29`, a genuine prefix of
the naive walk, not a different problem — and its rate there projected over the full
count. It sustains **5.83 × 10⁷ candidates/s** on `09_large_layered_feeds` and
**3.59 × 10⁸/s** on `10_large_cyclic_clusters`, the second being faster only because
almost every candidate is rejected on its first test.

**The projection does not depend on how good the control is.** An empty loop that
does nothing per candidate but produce it runs at **1.99 × 10⁹/s** on this machine,
and nothing that enumerates subsets can beat that floor. At that floor the two
recipes still need 10^9022 s and 10^6011 s — about 10^9005 and 10^5994 times the age
of the universe. Against the 5 s Rust/Go/C budget the margin is 10^9021 and 10^6010,
and no constant factor, vector width, or core count moves a number of that shape.

**Where the wall actually sits** is worth knowing, because it says what this
challenge is not: 5 s at that enumeration floor is about 10^10 candidates, so the
naive walk dies somewhere around `n = 33`. Everything above that is out of reach
forever, and everything the seeded recipes do — 20000 and 30000 strategies, tens of
thousands of prerequisites — is comfortable for one max flow. `rotten/main.py` itself
manages 169 candidates/s on `09_large_layered_feeds`, where the low-numbered
strategies have no prerequisites so almost every early candidate is valid and gets
fully scored, and 5.0 × 10⁵/s on `10_large_cyclic_clusters`, where a cycle rejects
nearly everything immediately. `make bench` does not wait for either: the runner
kills the process group at the timeout.

**The two recipes fail differently on purpose.** `09_large_layered_feeds` is a pure
acyclic graph whose bottom layer is nothing but cost, drawn small enough that a
shared prerequisite is worth paying for once and not twice, so about half the
ceiling survives and the cut runs right through the middle of the graph.
`10_large_cyclic_clusters` is the opposite: every one of its 4000 clusters is a
prerequisite cycle, so its five members are all-or-nothing however their
contributions fall, and clusters drag whole other clusters in across ranks. A
solver that collapses loops by hand, or that assumes prerequisites point one way
through the numbering, clears the first and not the second.
