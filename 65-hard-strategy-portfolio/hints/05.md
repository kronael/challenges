# Sources

- **Kleinberg & Tardos, *Algorithm Design*, §7.11 — "Project Selection"** is the
  canonical textbook treatment, and it derives the network in `03.md` arc by arc,
  including the argument that the minimum cut's source side is a valid selection.
  Their §7.2 (max-flow/min-cut) and §7.3 (choosing good augmenting paths) are the
  two sections behind it. Read §7.11 if only one thing here gets read.
- **J.-C. Picard, "Maximal closure of a graph and applications to combinatorial
  problems", *Management Science* 22(11), 1976, 1268–1272** is the paper that named
  the object. It proves the equivalence in general form — maximum-weight closure of
  a directed graph reduces to a minimum cut, and back — which is why the same
  network solves problems that look nothing like this one. J. M. W. Rhys, "A
  selection problem of shared fixed costs and network flows", *Management Science*
  17(3), 1970, 200–207, is the earlier special case: pick projects that share fixed
  costs, which is `12_star_shared_prerequisite` at scale.
- **Lerchs & Grossmann, "Optimum design of open-pit mines", CIM Bulletin 58, 1965**
  is where the problem comes from, and the origin explains the shape better than the
  finance framing does: a block of ore is worth mining only if every block above it
  is removed first, which is a prerequisite arc, and the pit outline is the closure.
  Textbooks still call this the open-pit mining problem.
- **CP-Algorithms — [Dinic's algorithm](https://cp-algorithms.com/graph/dinic.html)**
  is the reference implementation to compare against, including the level BFS, the
  per-node arc cursor, and the `i ^ 1` reverse-arc trick.
  [Ford–Fulkerson and Edmonds–Karp](https://cp-algorithms.com/graph/edmonds_karp.html)
  is worth reading alongside it for what the level structure buys.
- **USACO Guide — [Maximum Flow](https://usaco.guide/adv/max-flow)** and
  **[Minimum Cut](https://usaco.guide/adv/min-cut)**. The second module is the one
  that matters here: it covers exactly the move this challenge is built on, reading
  a selection problem as a cut and letting an infinite-capacity arc stand in for an
  implication. **[Codeforces EDU, ITMO Academy course 2](https://codeforces.com/edu/course/2)**
  has the same material as a graded flows section.
- **[POJ 2987 — Firing](http://poj.org/problem?id=2987)** is this exact problem in
  reverse dress: fire employees, each with a profit or a cost, where firing someone
  forces firing everyone who depends on them. It also asks for the size of the
  chosen set, which is the part this challenge leaves out — worth doing afterwards,
  because recovering the set from the residual network is the step that makes it
  concrete that the cut, not the flow, is the answer.
- **`39-hard-max-flow` in this repo** shares only the flow engine; `03.md` sets out
  why the two are different problems. If Dinic itself is the sticking point, do `39`
  first — it is the same algorithm with the network handed to you, so it isolates the
  implementation from the modelling. Its `hints/01.md` covers the residual graph and
  `hints/04.md` the arc cursor. Its source problem,
  [CSES 1694 — Download Speed](https://cses.fi/problemset/task/1694), is the
  plainest possible max-flow exercise.
- **`62-hard-neutral-basket`** is the useful contrast in the other direction, and
  `03.md` says why: a subset problem where the exponential search genuinely cannot
  be removed, only halved.
