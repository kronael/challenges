# Hint 1

> Spoilers. Open only when stuck, and only one file at a time.

**Two routes that share a venue share everything above it.** The route of `a` and
the route of `b` both end at the root, so once the two routes touch they are the
same sequence from that point up. The answer is where they first touch — and
after that point they never separate again.

So you are not looking for an overlap to measure. You are looking for a single
meeting point, and you can walk towards it from both ends at once.

One thing blocks walking in lockstep: the two venues are usually at different
distances from the root, so stepping both up by one does not keep them level.
What would you need to know about each venue before a lockstep walk makes sense?

**Level them first, and the lockstep walk cannot go wrong.** Say you know each
venue's distance from the root. Lift the deeper of the two venues by exactly the
difference, and both now stand at the same distance. Then step both up one venue
at a time:

- they cannot meet too early, because the first venue they agree on is by
  definition on both remaining routes, and
- they cannot slip past each other, because they stay at equal distance from the
  root at every step, and two venues at equal distance are either the same venue
  or have nothing in common yet.

Lifting the deeper venue first never skips the answer: the answer is never deeper
than the shallower venue of the pair, since it must lie on that venue's route.

The only thing this needs prepared in advance is the distance from the root, one
number per venue. It does not depend on the pairs at all, so it can be computed
once for the whole batch and reused by every pair. `parent` gives you no ordering
to lean on, though — a parent's number can be larger than its child's — so how do
you fill that array with one pass over the `n` venues, without recursing 200000
levels deep?
