# Complexity

**O(n) preprocessing.** The depth pass appends each venue to a chain exactly once
across the whole loop and writes each depth exactly once, so it is linear in `n`
regardless of how the venue numbers are arranged and regardless of the shape of
the hierarchy.

**O(distance) per pair, bounded by O(depth).** A pair costs the depth difference
plus the number of lockstep steps to the shared venue, so a pair deep in the same
subtree is answered in a few steps and only a pair that shares nothing but the
root walks a full route. Total: O(n + Σ distance), which is O(n + q · depth) in
the worst case.

**O(n) space** — the `depth` array, plus the answer. Nothing is allocated per
pair.

## The wall

The naive version in `rotten/main.py` is O(depth²) per pair, and both large
recipes are built so the shared venue sits near the root with both venues deep,
which is exactly its worst case:

| recipe | shape | n | pairs | depth | naive comparisons | golden |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `09_large_forked_chains` | root with two 99999-venue lines | 199999 | 150 | 99999 | ~1.1·10¹² | ~0.6 s |
| `10_large_shuffled_fan` | 1000-venue spine, then 25 branches of 7960, venue numbers shuffled | 200000 | 1800 | 8959 | ~1.1·10¹¹ | ~1.7 s |

Measured: an optimized native (`cc -O2 -march=native`) transcription of the naive
version takes on the order of nine minutes on the first recipe and about a
minute on the second, against the 5 s Rust/Go solver timeout — comfortably two
orders of magnitude over on the first and roughly 11x on the easier of the two.
The Python naive version is far slower still. The intended solution does about
1.3·10⁷ pointer steps on either recipe, which is where the ~1 s golden times
come from.

Note what does *not* create the wall: total input size. Both recipes are about
1.3 MB of JSON. The gap is entirely the per-pair rescan.
