# Hint 2

**Floating-point addition is not associative.** `(a + b) + c` and
`a + (b + c)` round differently: with doubles, `(1e16 + 1) + 1` is `1e16`,
because each `+ 1` is lost to rounding on its own, while `1e16 + (1 + 1)` is
`1e16 + 2`. Eight lanes, each keeping its own sum and added together at the
end, are a different parenthesization of the same additions, so in general
they produce a different answer.

That is why the compiler keeps the loop scalar. It may not change the result
of your program, and splitting the sum could, so every `+` waits for the one
before it. `-ffast-math` (or `-fassociative-math`) is how a program gives it
permission; `make vec` builds without either, and refuses a `#pragma` or
attribute that would grant it.

But nothing stops *you* from writing eight sums. The question is whether that is
correct *here*. Read the constraints again: every dosage is a multiple of
`1/4096`, and no sum of them can exceed `2 × 200000`. How many bits does a
double need to hold such a sum exactly — and what does that mean for the order
the additions happen in?
