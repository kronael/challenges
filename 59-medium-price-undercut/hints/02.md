# Approach — monotonic stack

Keep a **stack of day indices whose answer is not known yet**, prices
non-decreasing from bottom to top. Both ends of the work happen at the top.

Scan `i` from `0` to `n-1`:

1. While the stack is non-empty and `prices[stack.top] > prices[i]`: pop that
   index `t` and write `answer[t] = i - t`. Day `i` is the first undercut of day
   `t`, because `t` stayed open through every day in between.
2. Push `i`.

Whatever is still on the stack at the end is never undercut, so those answers
stay `0` — initialise the answer array to zeros and you never write them at all.

**The comparison is strict, and only one side of it.** Popping on
`prices[top] >= prices[i]` answers a day with an *equal* settlement, which is
not an undercut: a plateau of one repeated price then reports `1 1 1 …` instead
of looking past the plateau to the first genuinely lower day. Equal prices must
stay on the stack and pile up on top of each other.
