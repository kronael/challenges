# Hint 1

> Spoilers. Open only when stuck, and only one file at a time.

**One day can settle many open questions at once.** Walk the series forward and
stand on day `j`. Day `j` undercuts every earlier day that is still waiting and
whose price is above `prices[j]` — not just one of them. Each of those earlier
days is answered on the spot, with the same `j`, and never needs to be looked at
again.

So the question is not "where does day `i` get undercut?" asked `n` separate
times. It is "which still-open days does today close?" asked once per day.

**The still-open days are already sorted, for free.** Suppose days `a < b` are
both still waiting for an undercut when you reach day `j`. Then `prices[a]`
cannot be above `prices[b]`: if it were, day `b` would itself have undercut day
`a` when `b` arrived, and `a` would not still be open.

So at every moment the open days, read oldest to newest, have non-decreasing
prices. That is the invariant to lean on: the days today can close are exactly
the newest ones, and the moment you reach an open day today does *not* undercut,
you can stop — every day older than that one is priced at or below it.

Which end do you add to, and which end do you take from?
