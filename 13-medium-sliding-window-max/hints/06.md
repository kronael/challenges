# Hint 6

**Cut the readings into blocks of `k`**, starting at `0`, `k`, `2k`, and so on.
A window is `k` readings long, so it never spans more than two blocks: the
window starting at `i` is the tail of `i`'s block, from `i` to the block's last
reading, followed by the head of the next block, up to `i + k − 1`. When `i` is
a multiple of `k` it is exactly one block.

So its maximum is the larger of two numbers you can tabulate in advance:

- `suf[i]` — the maximum from `i` to the end of `i`'s block
- `pre[j]` — the maximum from the start of `j`'s block to `j`

and the answer for window `i` is `max(suf[i], pre[i + k − 1])`. The last block
may be shorter than `k`, but a window that starts at `i ≤ n − k` always starts
in a full block, so a short last block only ever supplies `pre`.

`pre` is a running maximum that restarts at every block start, walking left to
right; `suf` is the same walking right to left. Neither looks at a value to
decide what to do next: every reading gets one compare, in every block, for
every input. Three linear passes, no deque.
