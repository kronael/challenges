# Hint 2

**Choose the pivot defensively**: a fixed pivot (first, last, or middle
element) makes an already-sorted or reverse-sorted log the O(n²) worst case,
and sorted-ish logs are exactly what real sessions produce. Pick the pivot
index at random, or take the median of three sampled positions, so no fixed
input shape is the bad case.

**Duplicates want a three-way partition**: split into `> pivot`, `== pivot`,
`< pivot` (Dutch national flag) rather than a Lomuto-style two-way split — the
textbook partition that sends every element equal to the pivot to one side.
Real execution logs cluster: tens of thousands of fills can share the same
slippage, and a two-way split of that kind keeps re-partitioning the
equal-value block, shrinking the live range by one element per round instead
of halving it. That is how quickselect degrades to O(n²) on duplicate
clusters, and it is worth two orders of magnitude on the large cases here.
Once the equal block sits in the middle, a rank landing inside it is answered
on the spot without another round.

**A handful of ranks, one array**: sort the ranks and reuse the array left
partitioned by the previous answer — the bounds established for an earlier
rank still hold, so later ranks search a shorter range.
