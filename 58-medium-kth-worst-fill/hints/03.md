# Approach — why the two obvious tools are wrong here

**Sorting the whole log** is O(n log n) and answers far more than was asked:
every one of the `n` ranks ordered when at most eight were requested. At
`n = 4·10⁵` that is `n·log₂n ≈ 18.6n` element visits against roughly `2n` for
expected-linear selection, and the whole ordering has to be materialized to
read one value out of it.

**A heap of size k** is O(n log k) with O(k) memory. That is the right tool
for a *stream* too large to hold, but this log is offline and already in
memory, and the ranks asked for here are percentile ranks — `k` runs up to
`n`, so even after flipping the comparison to keep whichever side is smaller
the heap still holds 2·10⁵ entries, O(n log k) collapses back into
O(n log n), and each rank pays for its own allocation. Selection by
partitioning needs no auxiliary structure at all.
