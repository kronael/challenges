# Hint 1

> Spoilers. Open only when stuck, and only one file at a time.

**Every chain has exactly one highest desk.** Take the chain between `a` and `b`.
It climbs from `a` towards the top of the firm, turns around once, and descends to
`b`. That is forced: along the chain the depth falls and then rises and never
falls again, because a second turn would mean leaving a desk and later coming back
to it, and a chain visits each desk once. When `a` and `b` are the same desk, or
one sits above the other, the chain is a single straight run and its highest desk
is its upper end.

So a chain is not really a pair of desks. It is:

```
one desk v,  plus a run descending from v into left[v],  plus a run descending
             from v into right[v]   — either run may be empty
```

and every chain is described that way by exactly one `v`.

**That matters because the pairs are unenumerable and the desks are not.** There
are `n(n+1)/2` pairs — `1.25 * 10^11` at `n = 500000`, out of reach even if
scoring one were free. But there are only `n` candidates for the highest desk, and
no chain is lost: whichever chain wins, its own highest desk is the one that
proposes it. So

```
answer = the best, over every desk v, of
         pnl[v] + (best run descending into left[v], or nothing at all)
                + (best run descending into right[v], or nothing at all)
```

`n` candidates instead of `1.25 * 10^11` pairs, and the reduction cost nothing —
it is a different way of listing the same chains, not an approximation.

The phrase **"or nothing at all"** is doing real work in that formula, and it is
worth staring at before going further: what does a desk want from a sub-desk whose
whole branch lost money?
