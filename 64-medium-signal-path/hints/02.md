# Hint 2

**A desk needs one number from each sub-desk, and it needs to use that number two
different ways.** Write `down[c]` for the best total of a chain that starts at desk
`c` and only ever descends — never sideways, never back up. Then hint 1's formula
becomes two lines rather than one, and the difference between them is the whole
exercise:

```
gain(c)   = max(0, down[c])          # a missing sub-desk also gains 0
meets[v]  = pnl[v] + gain(left[v]) + gain(right[v])   # v is the highest desk
down[v]   = pnl[v] + max(gain(left[v]), gain(right[v]))
answer    = the largest meets[v] over every desk v
```

`meets[v]` is a **candidate answer**. `down[v]` is the number `v`'s own parent
will ask for. They are built from the same two child numbers and they combine them
differently on purpose.

**Why `down` may take only one arm.** A chain that arrives at `v` from above has
already spent the link between `v` and its parent. From `v` it can carry on into
one sub-desk, and that is it — reaching into both sub-desks would mean passing
through `v` twice, and then it is no longer a chain, it is two chains sharing a
desk. `meets[v]` is the case where the chain does not continue upward at all, and
that is exactly when both arms are available. So a desk with two profitable arms
hands its parent the better arm and keeps the other one for its own candidate:
`18_one_arm_travels_up` is a five-desk firm where adding both arms into `down`
reports `1201` instead of `1151`, and `01`, `15_shuffled_numbering` and
`19_wide_shuffled` catch it too.

**Why the `max(0, ...)`.** A chain is allowed to *stop* at `v`. So a sub-desk whose
best descending chain is a loss is worth nothing, not worth its loss — you decline
it and the chain simply ends. Written without the clamp, `meets[v]` and `down[v]`
both pay for branches nobody would take: `14_negative_arm_left_out` is a desk
whose right arm holds a genuinely profitable desk under a heavy loss, so the arm's
total is negative while its interior is not, and the unclamped answer is `53`
instead of `58`. `04`, `07` and `17_zero_contributions` fail the same way. Note
that the clamp applies to what comes *from* a sub-desk and never to `pnl[v]`
itself: `v` is on its own chain unconditionally, and `02`, `03` and `06` — firms
where every desk lost money — are what happens when you forget that.

**Three more things the fixtures are watching for.**

- **The answer is the best `meets[v]` over all `v`, not `meets[root]` or
  `down[root]`.** The winning chain is very often nowhere near the top:
  `16_negative_root_barrier` has a root so bad that the whole answer lives inside
  one arm, and `11_bend_at_internal_desk` turns at a desk one level down. Ten of
  the seventeen fixtures catch an answer read off the root alone.
- **A chain holds at least one desk.** Seeding the running best at `0` instead of
  at the first candidate quietly permits the empty chain, and every all-negative
  firm then answers `0`: `02`, `03` and `06`.
- **The desk numbering tells you nothing.** A sub-desk's number may be below its
  own desk's, and `root` need not be desk `0`. Sweeping the desks in index order,
  or in reverse index order, and hoping parents come after children is wrong on
  `15_shuffled_numbering` and `19_wide_shuffled`.

Which leaves the ordering question. `down[v]` needs `down` for both sub-desks
before it can be written, and the numbering will not give you that order. What
*will*, and what does it cost — remembering that the firm may be one 500000-desk
line?
