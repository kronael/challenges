# Hint 2

**Number the venues in the order you first reach them, and the links sort
themselves into two kinds.** Walk out from some venue, giving each venue a number
the moment you first arrive at it. Every link is then either

- a *branch link*, the one you used to arrive at a fresh venue, or
- a link leading to a venue that already has a number.

The second kind is where the loops are. Such a link joins the venue you are
standing on to a venue already on the path behind you, closing a loop: that link
plus the branch links between its two ends. Every loop in the network shows up
this way, so between them the second kind of link accounts for every loop there
is.

Now look at one branch link — the one you used to enter venue `v`. Everything you
reach after entering `v` and before backing out of it is *below* `v`. That branch
link lies on a loop exactly when something at or below `v` has a link of the
second kind landing on a venue numbered lower than `v` — that is, on `v`'s parent
or further back up the path. Otherwise nothing below `v` gets back up except
through the branch link itself, and the link is critical.

So each venue needs one summary number: the lowest venue number reachable from at
or below it, using branch links downward plus at most one link of the second kind.
That summary is computable as you back out of a venue, from its own number and the
summaries of the venues directly below it. One walk, two numbers per venue, and
every branch link gets classified on the way out.

Two details bite if you are sloppy. First: the link you arrived on is not a link
of the second kind — but when two links join the same pair of venues, "the link I
arrived on" is one specific entry of `links`, not "any link back to that venue".
Second: the network can be a 200000-venue line, so the walk had better not be
200000 call frames deep.
