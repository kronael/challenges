# Complexity

**O(n + m) time.** Building the flat adjacency is one pass to count degrees, one
prefix sum, and one pass over `links` to fill the slots. The walk then visits each
venue once — pushed once, popped once — and examines each of the `2m` adjacency
slots exactly once, because each venue's cursor only moves forward. Sorting the
answer adds `O(k log k)` on the `k` critical links, which is bounded by `m` and
never dominates.

**O(n + m) space.** The `start`, `order`, `reach`, `entered` and `cursor` arrays
are one integer per venue; `slot_to` and `slot_link` are `2m` integers; the stack
holds at most `n` venues. No container is allocated per venue or per link.

**Depth is data, not stack.** The stack is a heap array that happens to hold the
current path, so a 200000-venue line costs 200000 array entries instead of 200000
call frames.

## The wall

`rotten/main.py` re-counts the connected groups of venues from scratch for every
link: `O(m · (n + m))` against the intended `O(n + m)`, one pass versus `m + 1`
passes.

| recipe | shape | n | m | critical | peak depth | golden | naive, native |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `09_large_cycle_chain` | 66666 three-venue loops strung out in a line, neighbours joined by one link | 199998 | 266663 | 66665 | 199998 | 0.43 s | ~640 s |
| `10_large_ring_and_hangers` | 120000-venue ring with 40000 links thrown across it, 50000 venues hung off it in trees, 5000 of those doubled, plus a detached 30000-venue line; venue numbers and link order shuffled | 200000 | 244999 | 74999 | 69923 | 0.94 s | ~3900 s |

Measured: an optimized native (`cc -O2 -march=native`) transcription of
`rotten/main.py` scans 300 of the links in 0.72 s and 4.78 s respectively, which
projects the full scans to about 640 s and 3900 s; both were still running when
killed at a hard 30 s cap. Against the 5 s Rust/Go solver timeout that is a margin
of roughly 130x on the easier recipe. `golden/main.py` — *Python* — is the 0.43 s
and 0.94 s column, and both inputs are under 4 MB of JSON, so essentially all of
the gap is the repeated traversal rather than parsing.

The two recipes are deliberately different failures. `09_large_cycle_chain` is the
depth case: the walk is 199998 venues deep and half the network hangs behind every
joining link, so a recursive solution dies and the naive scan pays for a
full-network traversal on every one of its 266663 links. `10_large_ring_and_hangers`
is the identity case: no order to lean on, a big loop with no critical links inside
it, 5000 doubled links that a parent-venue check reports as critical, and a second
disconnected piece that a single-root walk never visits at all. Its shuffled venue
numbers also cost the naive scan about 6.6x more per link than the tidy first
recipe, purely in cache misses.
