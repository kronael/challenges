# Sources

- [LeetCode 1192 — Critical Connections in a Network](https://leetcode.com/problems/critical-connections-in-a-network/)
  is this problem, at a tenth of the size, with a connected graph, no parallel
  edges, and the answer given as vertex pairs rather than edge positions.
- R. E. Tarjan, *A note on finding the bridges of a graph*, Information Processing
  Letters 2(6), 1974, pp. 160–161 — the two-line criterion this challenge is built
  on, in its original form.
- J. Hopcroft and R. E. Tarjan, *Algorithm 447: Efficient algorithms for graph
  manipulation*, Communications of the ACM 16(6), 1973, pp. 372–378 — the same
  single-pass low-link machinery applied to biconnected components, which is where
  the articulation-point variant of the test comes from.
- CP-Algorithms — *Finding bridges in a graph in O(N+M)*: the recursive
  formulation, plus the multi-edge caveat spelled out. Its *Finding bridges
  online* companion is the incremental version, for when links arrive one at a
  time instead of all at once.
- CLRS, *Introduction to Algorithms*, 3rd edition, Problem 22-2 — articulation
  points, bridges, and biconnected components as a guided exercise off the
  depth-first-search chapter. Good for seeing why the bridge test uses `>` and the
  articulation-point test uses `>=`.
- The USACO Guide's advanced graph section covers biconnected and 2-edge-connected
  components: where to go once bridges are second nature, since contracting the
  2-edge-connected pieces turns any network into the tree of its critical links.
