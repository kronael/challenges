# Complexity — the same comparisons, four windows at a time

`golden/solution.c` and hint 5's sliding window compare every window with every
guide as one XOR of two words. The golden builds every window's word once,
`O(genome × L)`; the sliding window rebuilds them for each guide as it slides,
`O(guides × genome)`. Both are `O((guides + L) × genome)` time: there is no
asymptotic gap to hide behind. The golden wins because its window loop carries
nothing from one window to the next, so four windows share each instruction;
the sliding window makes every window wait for the previous one.

Measured on this box — Ryzen 9 5950X, two cores visible and shared with other
jobs, `-O3 -march=x86-64-v3`, the two `make bench` inputs (10^6 bases, 2000
guides), `solve` alone, best of 14 calls:

| build | `golden/` | sliding window |
|---|---|---|
| gcc 12.2, `46_large_many` | 0.81 s | 2.38 s |
| gcc 12.2, `46_large_repeat` | 0.81 s | 2.35 s |
| clang 21, `46_large_many` | 0.75 s | 2.56 s |
| clang 21, `46_large_repeat` | 0.76 s | 2.41 s |

About three times faster, for the same comparisons. `c/`'s `make bench` builds
for baseline x86-64 at `-O2`, where the golden's `solve` takes about 3.3 s of
its 5 seconds a case on an idle machine.

`make vec` sees the same thing without a clock. Traced to the end on `vec.in`,
the sliding window retires 68.0 scalar instructions per base under gcc and 79.9
under clang — about 17 and 20 per window and guide, times four guides — and
`golden/solution.c` 0.34 and 0.45. `-mtune=skylake` changes neither verdict.
