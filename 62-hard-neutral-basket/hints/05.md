# Sources

- [LeetCode 1755 — Closest Subsequence Sum](https://leetcode.com/problems/closest-subsequence-sum/)
  is this problem with smaller numbers: `n <= 40`, `|nums[i]| <= 10^7`,
  `|goal| <= 10^9`. Same technique, and its editorial covers both the binary
  search and the two-pointer merge of the two halves.
- E. Horowitz and S. Sahni, *Computing partitions with applications to the
  knapsack problem*, Journal of the ACM 21(2), 1974, pp. 277–292 — the original
  meet-in-the-middle: split, enumerate both halves, sort one, and search it.
  Everything else here is that paper.
- R. Schroeppel and A. Shamir, *A T = O(2^{n/2}), S = O(2^{n/4}) algorithm for
  certain NP-complete problems*, SIAM Journal on Computing 10(3), 1981,
  pp. 456–464 — the same time bound with a quarter of the exponent in space,
  by generating each half's nets in sorted order on demand from two quarters.
  Worth knowing for the version of this problem where `2^(n/2)` nets no longer
  fit in memory.
- R. E. Korf, *A complete anytime algorithm for number partitioning*, Artificial
  Intelligence 106(2), 1998, pp. 181–203, and N. Karmarkar and R. M. Karp, *The
  differencing method of set partitioning*, UCB/CSD 82/113, 1982 — the
  branch-and-bound line of attack on the balanced-partition case, and why its
  running time depends on the data in a way meet in the middle does not.
- `17-medium-knapsack` in this repo is the contrast worth studying: same "choose
  a subset" shape, but with a bounded total it becomes a table indexed by total,
  in `O(n · capacity)`. The two techniques are chosen by the *range* of the
  values, not by the shape of the question. LeetCode 494 (Target Sum) and 1049
  (Last Stone Weight II) are the same question again at small value ranges, where
  the table is the right answer.
- G. J. Woeginger, *Exact algorithms for NP-hard problems: a survey*, LNCS 2570,
  2003, pp. 185–207 — places meet in the middle among the "moderately
  exponential" algorithms, the family whose win is halving the exponent rather
  than removing it, and lists which other NP-hard problems the split applies to.
