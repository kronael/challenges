# Hint 2

**Cut the desk in two and the choices multiply instead of adding up.** Say
positions `0 .. 18` are the front half and positions `19 .. 37` the back half.
Every basket in the whole problem is then *exactly* one choice from the front
plus one choice from the back, and the two choices are completely independent:
nothing you do in the front constrains the back.

That factoring is the point. `2^38 = 2^19 * 2^19`. The baskets are a product of
two small sets, so you never hold the product — you hold the two factors, 524288
nets each, and between them they account for all 275 billion baskets. Both halves
include their own empty choice, so the empty basket (front empty, back empty) and
the full basket (front all, back all) fall out of the pairing for free; neither
needs special-casing.

Now the problem has changed shape. You have two lists of nets, `front` and
`back`, and you want to minimise `|f + b - target|` over all pairs. Rewrite that
as

```
minimise |b - (target - f)|
```

and read it again. For one fixed `f` you are not searching for a *pair* any more.
You are asking one question about the `back` list on its own: **which entry of
`back` sits closest to the number `target - f`?** Answer that question 524288
times, once per `f`, and the smallest answer you saw is the result.

So: 524288 nearest-value queries against a fixed list of 524288 numbers that
never changes between queries. Nearest needs *order*, not membership — a hash set
answers "is `target - f` in there", which is only the distance-0 case, and the
mandate is usually unreachable. Prepare the `back` list once so each query costs
a logarithm instead of a scan, and the whole job is a few million operations on a
38-number input.

Two details will bite. Nearest means looking **both ways**: the closest entry is
either the first one at or above `target - f` or the last one below it, and some
inputs only ever have one of the two (fixture `05` puts every query below the
whole list, fixture `07` puts every query above it). And with 19 positions of
`10^12` on one side the running nets pass `10^13`, so every accumulator, the
subtraction, and the distance are 64-bit.
